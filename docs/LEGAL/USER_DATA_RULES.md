# User data responsibilities and public-content rules

**v1.0.5 — proposed terms for an actual operator's validly accepted service agreement.** The safety rules describe the intended workflow. Contractual duties and remedies require proper adoption and local review. They add no MIT license conditions, do not amend earlier agreements and do not bind non-consenting people or regulators.

## What users must do

1. **Submit only public-safe content.** Do not add your own or another person's personal information, confidential work or secrets to public text, references, images, ENS/IPFS/NFT metadata or support posts. Required protocol addresses/identities remain visible; do not attach identifying profiles. Consent does not override this service's content restriction.
2. **Check everything you control.** Review source material, filenames, document properties, image metadata, query strings, embedded links and AI outputs. Do not publish access tokens, private sharing links or content exposing another person. A checkbox is not a scanner.
3. **Have authority for private work.** Before collecting or privately sharing protected information, establish required authority, lawful basis, notices, permissions and safeguards. Agree actual roles and required processing arrangements. Do not assume the publisher or owner supplies storage, legal clearance, consent, identity screening or a data-processing agreement.
4. **Control agents and providers.** Apply the same rules to automated agents, limit their access and sharing, check outputs and secure accounts. Review pinning, RPC, hosting and AI providers you select.
5. **Report and contain mistakes.** Promptly use the actual operator's private contact with a minimal job/reference description. Stop further dissemination and cooperate proportionately with lawful containment and rights handling. Preserve only necessary restricted evidence and records required by law.

## Maximum lawful allocation to the submitting user

An operator may adopt this clause after review:

> To the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, you are responsible for the data, content, instructions and disclosures you select or authorize, including those made by agents acting for you. You undertake to follow the public-content rules, obtain required rights and authorizations, and perform privacy obligations attaching to your own activities. You must not submit personal information, confidential material or secrets to the public workflow or instruct others to do so. You remain responsible for your chosen private workflow and providers to the extent the law assigns you that responsibility. Your submission does not appoint MONTREAL.AI, software contributors or the contract owner as your private-data custodian, processor, adviser or guarantor merely because their software or contract is used.

This cannot transfer another party's statutory accountability, excuse its conduct, change factual controller/processor status, supply missing notices/consent or waive data-subject rights. Users may have different roles for different operations; do not automatically label every user a sole controller.

## Recovery for a business user's breach

Where enforceable, separately agree recovery from a professional/business user of reasonable, evidenced third-party losses and legally recoverable response costs caused by its unlawful disclosure or breach. Identify beneficiaries precisely; these may include the actual operator, owner and MONTREAL.AI where an enforceable arrangement permits it. Limit recovery to the user's responsibility. Exclude the protected party's own breach or contribution, unlawful consumer burdens, and fines/liabilities that cannot legally be indemnified. Specify notice, defense control, mitigation, proportional cooperation and settlement consent. Local advice must confirm scope and enforceability.

This is potential recovery between contracting parties, not immunity from claims, compensation, investigation, regulator powers or mandatory reporting. No automatic charge, forfeiture or seizure of escrow, bonds or reserved claims is authorized. Code-supported exits and mandatory remedies remain.

## If a user breaks the rules

Subject to actual authority, law and valid terms, an operator may refuse new prohibited submissions, restrict an interface it controls, and remove or unpin controlled content. This cannot erase third-party pins or chain history, or censor every direct contract call. Use existing powers only within their limits, protect accrued rights and preserve settlement/recovery. Follow the [privacy response procedure](../OPERATIONS/PRIVACY_RESPONSE.md).
