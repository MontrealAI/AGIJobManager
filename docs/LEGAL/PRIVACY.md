# Privacy and public-data notice

**v1.0.4 software disclosure.** A deployment operator or host must publish its own notice for the data it actually processes. This document does not identify an unconfigured deployment's data controller or establish compliance.

| Surface | What to understand |
| --- | --- |
| Ethereum, ENS and content references | Wallets, names, job details/URIs, events, fees, votes and transactions can be public, linkable and replicated. Wallets are not necessarily anonymous; hashes and IPFS identifiers can link information. |
| Standalone browser storage | Configuration, labels, wallet/network hints, job notes and completion drafts can persist on the device. Clear site data or use clearing controls as appropriate. Shared-device users may read saved information. v1.0.4 does not restore terms acknowledgement from storage. |
| Wallet, RPC, host and dependencies | Wallets/RPCs process accounts and transactions. Hosts, CDNs, explorers and metadata services may receive IP addresses, requests or logs. External job content can contact its provider. Single-file does not mean offline or no third-party processing. |
| Broader UI and integrations | Wallet connectors, hosted/API routes, proof services, support and operator-added analytics have their own processing. Assess the actual configuration. |
| Repository and support | Issues, commits and attachments may be public and persistent. Never post keys, identity documents, confidential work, privileged advice or private user evidence. Use the security reporting route for vulnerabilities. |

The acknowledgement checkbox gates this console's write flow locally; it sends no consent receipt to MONTREAL.AI and does not prove every participant accepted an operator's terms. Wallets, hosts and RPCs can still process information as described above. Direct contract access bypasses the console.

Keep confidential work in an access-controlled system and grant reviewers only needed access. Do not put personal or sensitive information directly in job details, ENS text, public URLs, NFT metadata, transactions or public IPFS objects. Encryption or hashing alone does not remove privacy duties. Deleting a note, disabling a UI or revoking an ENS record cannot erase prior public copies.

Collect only what a defined purpose or legal duty requires. Specify retention, access controls, incident procedures and a real privacy contact. Account for mandatory records, lawful holds and rights requests without promising impossible deletion. This release introduces no central identity-document collection or telemetry.
