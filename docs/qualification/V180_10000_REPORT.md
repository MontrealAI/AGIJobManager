# AGI Jobs: 10,000-offer improvement experiment

22 September 2026 · Synthetic, matched scenarios · 180 days per cohort

**Decision: keep the non-retainer baseline. Neither candidate passed all frozen improvement constraints.** Retainers protect reviewers but reduced useful output and increased employer/Agent losses in this workload. Additional issuer capacity did not increase useful output and raised peak liabilities. The useful next step is measured task/runtime qualification and bounded review exposure, not automatic promotion of either policy.

## What counts as an AGI Job?

Any lawful work a person or team can perform using a keyboard and mouse while watching a screen. Examples include operating business software, creating editable designs, repairing software, reconciling records, managing permitted workflows, conducting research and independently testing another worker's results. These are illustrations, not a supported-professions list. Each instance needs an objective, environment, authority, inputs, constraints, economic budget, acceptance tests, evidence, deadline and settlement/recovery rules.

This experiment executes financial and scheduling events. It does not execute the represented applications, produce 10,000 artifacts or observe 10,000 human-equivalent outcomes. Job labels are illustrative overlays and do not establish capability. The supplied current runtime supports isolated public work; private accounts and long team projects require separately commissioned adapters/stages.

## Qualification and recovery work

Issuer lanes are employer / worker / recovery. The baseline uses 20,000 employer units/day, 50,000 worker units/day and a reserved recovery lane at one eighth of worker capacity. Queues are bounded at 512/1,024/128 units; requests beyond the queue/deadline wait limit are rejected and retried with deterministic jitter. A day-50–53 outage remains modeled. Repeated qualification can materially increase accepted units; request counts are not distinct jobs.

## Bounded improvement and held-out evaluation

The protocol was written before execution. Selection maximizes useful settlements subject to no deterioration in useful output, peak combined liabilities or total USDC losses across all negative engagements. Both alternatives failed at least one constraint. The selected policy therefore remains baseline. This is one bounded policy-improvement cycle, not recursive model training or proof of autonomous self-improvement.

It produced 1,739 useful settlements, the same as baseline, with higher peak liabilities and slightly higher total losses. No candidate was promoted.

## Workload and reproducibility

10,000 original offers were sampled without replacement, stratified by the six original price levels, and split into disjoint 5,000-offer calibration/evaluation cohorts (seed 18020260922). Price proportions are 40/25/15/10/7/3%. Original input flags, attempt counts, economic profiles and eligibility are retained. Actors are remapped into one 2,560-participant logical pool per cohort, arrivals span 76 days and events run to day 180. This changes load and identity geography relative to the million-offer experiment: compare policies within this study, not raw totals against the old study.

Frozen synthetic cost, value, duration and failure assumptions were retained. No recent-model capability uplift was invented. The general computer-work profile changes what a job can express; it does not validate these economic assumptions. There is one seed and one configuration, so there is no robust statistical claim about other workloads, rare incidents, correlated failures, market demand or hardware throughput.

Five runs completed with **1,997,604 independently replayed events**, representing 25,000 scenario-offer executions over 10,000 unique offers. Independent summary, per-role conservation, qualification, issuer, retainer state and retainer gas comparisons passed. Source, exact inputs, settings, per-job rows, hashes and replay records are in the compact experiment package.

## Engineering actions supported by this evidence

1. Offer retainer and explicitly risk-budgeted non-retainer review as separate, signed policy choices. Preserve existing earned credits and journals. Never forecast a retainer that is not funded.
2. Bound attempts and review spending before processing. Unpaid work is a real risk that cannot be removed by renaming a budget or by showing aggregate positive margins.
3. Qualify the exact model/tool/image configuration on realistic computer tasks and independently corrupted deliverables. Include all failures, abstentions, human interventions and actual invoices.
4. Measure the complete issuer/RPC/evidence pipeline and optimize queue work before adding capacity. In this smaller workload, more issuer capacity did not improve useful output.
5. Test a new candidate against fresh held-out work after revising its design. Simulation evidence alone must not loosen financial limits or enable production wallets.
