# Privacy setup and incident response

Use with the [privacy notice](../LEGAL/PRIVACY.md). This guide is not a legal opinion or a promise that public data can be erased.

## Set up once for the actual service

Record privately the operator, roles for each processing activity, providers and jurisdictions, permitted content, required agreements, retention/deletion rules, least-privilege access and a working privacy contact. Publish the required notice through the actual operator's service. Do not appoint MONTREAL.AI as an independent deployment's contact without agreement. Minimize logs and identification collection. Reassess material changes.

## Handle an exposure

1. **Receive privately.** Ask only for a job/transaction reference, affected service and brief description. Do not ask for the exposed dataset, identity documents or secrets in a public issue. Verify rights requests proportionately through a private route.
2. **Contain controlled copies.** Stop further publication, restrict off-chain access, revoke exposed links/credentials and remove controlled copies when lawful. Preserve only necessary restricted evidence and mandatory records. Consider an intake pause; a settlement pause can obstruct recovery and is not an eraser.
3. **Assess promptly.** Identify responsible parties, affected people/data, recipients and risks. Route to the actual privacy lead/adviser. Do not wait for an indemnity dispute or assume user fault eliminates the operator's duties.
4. **Notify/respond where required.** Apply relevant thresholds and deadlines. GDPR controllers generally notify the authority within 72 hours of awareness unless a breach is unlikely to risk people's rights/freedoms; processors notify controllers without undue delay. High-risk breaches can require notice to people. Document lawful exceptions/delays. [GDPR Articles 33–34](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng).
5. **Remediate and record.** Handle applicable rights requests, coordinate with providers, restrict/delete controlled data as legally appropriate and explain actual limits. Record the outcome, retention date and preventive change. Unpinning or revoking ENS access cannot guarantee erasure of others' copies.

A wallet transaction does not waive privacy rights. A no-personal-data rule does not automatically defeat a rights request. Do not promise 24/7 support or invent service commitments; applicable legal deadlines remain. Allocate work accurately and maintain the smallest process that meets the actual obligations.
